Unified Startup Marketing Framework
This document serves as a strategic blueprint for startups to define their market position, execute high-impact campaigns, and build a sustainable growth engine.
Phase 1: The Strategic Foundation (STP)
Before spending a dollar on ads, you must define who you are talking to and why they should care.
1. Segmentation
Divide the broad market into manageable groups based on:
 * Demographics/Firmographics: Age, income, industry, company size.
 * Psychographics: Values, pain points, motivations, and professional goals.
 * Behavioral: How they currently solve the problem and their tech stack.
2. Targeting (The ICP)
Identify your Ideal Customer Profile (ICP). Ask:
 * Which segment has the highest "willingness to pay"?
 * Which segment is easiest to reach with your current resources?
 * Which segment has the shortest sales cycle?
3. Positioning
Craft a Unique Value Proposition (UVP) using the formula:
> "We help [Target Audience] do [Key Action/Benefit] by [Unique Mechanism], unlike [Primary Competitor/Alternative]."
> 
Phase 2: Channel Prioritization (The Bullseye Framework)
Startups fail when they try to be everywhere at once. Use Gabriel Weinberg’s Bullseye Framework to find your "core" channel.
 * The Outer Ring (The Possible): Brainstorm every possible channel (SEO, Viral, PR, Offline Ads, Social Ads, Content, etc.).
 * The Middle Ring (The Probable): Run low-cost, 2-week experiments in the top 3 channels.
 * The Inner Ring (The Bullseye): Double down on the one channel that shows the lowest Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) and highest scalability.
Phase 3: The Growth Engine (AARRR Metrics)
Once you have a channel, map the customer journey using "Pirate Metrics" to identify where you are losing people.
| Stage | Goal | Key Question | Sample KPI |
|---|---|---|---|
| Acquisition | Get people to the "store" | Where are our users coming from? | Traffic, CTR |
| Activation | The "Aha!" moment | Do users have a great first experience? | Sign-up Rate, Onboarding % |
| Retention | Keep them coming back | Do they stay active over 30/60/90 days? | Churn Rate, DAU/MAU |
| Referral | Turn users into advocates | Do they tell their friends? | Net Promoter Score (NPS) |
| Revenue | Capture value | Are we making more than we spend? | LTV, ARPU, CAC Payback |
Phase 4: From Funnel to Loop (Sustainable Scaling)
While funnels (AARRR) are great for tracking, Growth Loops are what create compounding returns. Instead of just "pouring" more money into the top of the funnel, design systems where the output of one cycle becomes the input for the next.
 * Example (Viral Loop): New User \rightarrow Uses Product \rightarrow Invites Colleague \rightarrow New User.
 * Example (Content Loop): New Content \rightarrow Indexed by Google \rightarrow Attracts User \rightarrow User creates UGC \rightarrow New Content.
Phase 5: Measurement & North Star Metric
Identify your North Star Metric (NSM)—the single metric that best captures the core value your product delivers to customers.
 * Airbnb: Nights Booked.
 * Slack: Messages Sent.
 * WhatsApp: Number of Messages Sent.
The Golden Ratio: Aim for an LTV:CAC ratio of 3:1 (Lifetime Value is 3x the Acquisition Cost).
Implementation Checklist:
 * [ ] Define your ICP and UVP.
 * [ ] Run 3 channel experiments via Bullseye.
 * [ ] Map your Activation "Aha!" moment.
 * [ ] Set up a dashboard tracking AARRR daily.
 * [ ] Identify your North Star Metric.
